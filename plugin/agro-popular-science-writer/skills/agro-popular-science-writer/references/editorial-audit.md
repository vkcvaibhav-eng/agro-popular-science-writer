# Selected-framework editorial audit

Use this only after an article has been drafted. Treat the named author as an editorial lens, not as a persona to impersonate. Never claim that the author reviewed, approved, or wrote the article, and never score exact voice imitation.

## Audit and revision sequence

1. Freeze the verified evidence map and reference metadata. Do not introduce a new factual claim during editing unless it is supported and cited.
2. Re-read the selected author's framework in [editorial-frameworks.md](editorial-frameworks.md). Audit the draft against all four framework rules.
3. Audit scientific accuracy, claim–source matching, uncertainty, terminology, article length, subject-first focus, and APA 7 consistency.
4. Revise weak passages, transitions, technical explanations, opening, and ending. Remove report-like prose, unsupported drama, unnecessary location framing, and generic filler.
5. Run a final fact lock: confirm that the revision has not changed the meaning of a source, added an unsupported inference, broken a citation, or altered verified bibliographic metadata.
6. Score the revised article using the rubric below. Score honestly from observable features in the final text; do not raise a score merely to make the result appear successful.

Do not expose hidden reasoning or a line-by-line internal critique. Show the revised final article and a concise evidence-based audit.

## Framework-alignment rubric

| Criterion | Maximum | What earns the score |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Opening and hook | 10 | The opening applies the selected framework quickly, accurately, and without unsupported drama. |
| Sentence construction and cadence | 15 | Sentence length, paragraph rhythm, and transitions serve the framework while remaining clear and original. |
| Narrative structure and flow | 15 | The article follows the selected framework's movement and maintains a coherent story arc. |
| Vocabulary and technical explanation | 15 | Terms are precise, accessible, and explained through concrete language rather than jargon. |
| Research translation | 15 | Methods and findings are converted into readable meaning without simplifying away important conditions. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | 10 | Claims are cited, strength is proportionate to evidence, and limitations are visible. |
| Subject and agricultural relevance | 10 | The scientific subject remains central and its agricultural significance is clear without forced regional or farmer framing. |
| Ending | 10 | The ending fulfils the selected framework and closes with justified significance, consequence, or uncertainty. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Rating bands

- **90–100 — Excellent alignment:** the framework is consistently visible and the article is publication-ready after ordinary human review.
- **80–89 — Strong alignment:** the framework is clear, with minor unevenness.
- **70–79 — Recognizable alignment:** the approach is present but several passages remain generic or structurally weak.
- **60–69 — Partial alignment:** only some framework features are sustained.
- **Below 60 — Weak alignment:** the draft does not yet reliably apply the selected framework.

A low score does not authorize score inflation. If the revised article remains below 80, explain the limiting factor in the editorial verdict.

## Required audit display

After the complete final article and reference list, present:

1. `## Editorial audit — [author name]: [framework name]`
2. A compact table with `Criterion`, `Score`, `Maximum`, and one brief `Evidence in the final article` statement.
3. `Overall framework alignment: NN/100 — [rating band]`.
4. A two- or three-sentence `Editorial verdict` identifying the strongest framework feature and the main remaining weakness.
5. A reminder that the score measures alignment with the high-level editorial framework, not similarity to the author's exact personal voice.
